Moon Peekaboo

The Sun always lights up half of the Moon. As the Moon travels around the Earth, we see more or less of that sunny half. That is why the Moon seems to change shape.

How the Moon plays peekaboo

  1. The Sun lights half

    Sunlight shines on the Moon from one side, like a flashlight on a ball. The side facing the Sun is bright and the other side is dark. The yellow ring marks the edge of the sunny half.

  2. The Moon goes around

    The Moon travels in a big circle around the Earth. One trip takes about 29½ days. That is about one month. The word month comes from moon.

  3. We only see our side

    From Earth we can only see the half of the Moon that faces us, inside the blue ring. The Moon's shape is the part of the sunny half that is on our side.

Try it with a lamp and a ball

In a dark room, switch on one lamp. That is the Sun. Hold a ball out at arm's length. The ball is the Moon and your head is the Earth. Turn slowly in a circle while you watch the ball. You will see a thin crescent, a half, and a full ball, just like the real Moon.

Sizes and distances are not to scale. The real Sun is about 400 times wider than the Moon, and about 400 times farther away.

Moon shapes are shown as seen from the northern half of the Earth. South of the equator the Moon looks flipped, left to right.

Dates use the average Moon trip of 29.53 days, so a phase shown here can be off by up to a day.
